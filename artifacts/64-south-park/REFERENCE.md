# South Park (64 South Park) — reference dossier

The as-built record for `artifacts/64-south-park/`. The plan is
`docs/asset-plans/64-south-park.md`; where this file and the plan disagree, **this
file is right and the plan is stale** (repo rule: REPORT beats plan). The build log,
including everything that was tried and rejected, is `REPORT.md`.

## What this is

San Francisco's oldest public park, laid out in 1852 by George Gordon and built from
1854 by the English architect George Goddard, in the block bounded by Second, Third,
Brannan and Bryant. Nothing of the original survives except the rounded kerbs — the
Regency row houses went in 1906, the cast-iron railing and the Dutch windmill went when
the city took the park over in 1897. What the asset models is the **2017 Fletcher Studio
re-cut**: one 188 m meandering promenade of oblong concrete tablets, six long curved
cast-in-place seat walls, five sloping lawns, thirteen bio-retention beds, and the
Berliner Seilfabrik **"Shout"** — a play sculpture that is a perfect circle in plan and
two undulating steel tubes in section.

## The terrain drape — read this before changing anything

**This asset is draped on the baked terrain, and that is why `min_z` is negative and
`targetHeightM` is 21.04 m.** Both are deliberate, both are asserted by
`validate_64_south_park.py`, and neither is a contract slip.

`placeGeneric()` in `app/src/assets.js` seats a landmark from a single terrain sample:

```js
const y = Math.max(0, data.sampleElevation(x, z));   // at the anchor, once
```

That is correct for a building — a compact footprint on a slope buries its base uphill,
which is what the real building does — and wrong for an asset that IS the ground. South
Park falls **6.106 m** over its 160 m (13.58 m at the Second Street end, 7.73 m at
Third). Built flat and seated at the anchor's 10.728 m, the model was buried 2.9 m at one
end and floating 3.2 m at the other; in the running app the whole north-east half of the
park had disappeared under the baked landcover, with only the tree crowns showing. That
is not visible in any Blender render — the asset looks perfect in isolation — and no
contract check catches it.

So every z in `build_64_south_park.py` is `authored height + dy(u)`, with `dy` sampled by
`sample_terrain.mjs` from the same heightmap the bake uses. The consequences:

- **z = 0 is the anchor's ground**, not the bottom of the model, which is exactly where
  the loader puts it. `min_z` is −4.18 m (the Third Street end plus the plate's skirt).
  The check that replaces "min_z ≈ 0" is that the ground plate stands 0.34 m above the
  terrain **along its whole length** — measured spread 0.0000 m.
- **`targetHeightM` is the vertical extent**, 21.0415 m, because the loader's scale is
  `targetHeightM / bbox height` and it must be 1.0. Confirmed in the app:
  `sf-assets: 64-south-park merged … uniform x1.0000 at 3830, -1281`.
- The cross-axis is flat to 0.296 m, measured, so `dy` is a function of `u` alone.

Anything else in this city that ships a large ground-plane asset will hit the same wall.
The alternative is a loader change that drapes at placement time, which is a bigger
decision than one landmark should take on its own.

## The height datum — read this before changing anything

`targetHeightM = 15.0` is the crest of the tallest American elm, and it is **estimated**.
No source consulted gives tree heights: the 2015 HortScience survey measured trunk
diameter only, OSM tags no height on any of the 20 nodes, and the 2017 works replaced 30
of the trees anyway. 15.0 m is inferred from DBH (elms 16½–33″), from the pollard-and-
topping history (attachments at 8–12 ft, topping at 12–15 ft, plus a decade of regrowth),
and from the aerial, where the canopy reads at or just below the roofs of the 3–4 storey
row around it — 181 South Park 16.5 m, 188 South Park 15.93 m, 171 South Park 12.6 m, all
measured and all already in the manifest.

**The error is contained.** Whatever the trees really are, the plan dimensions stay
exact: the loader scales by `targetHeightM / bbox height`, `targetHeightM` is set to the
measured bbox height, and the scale lands on 1.0. A wrong crest makes the trees wrong,
not the park. The manifest entry is `"estimated": true`, the crest is driven by
`TALLEST_ELM_M` in `extract_park_uv.py`, and `validate_64_south_park.py` asserts it to
10 mm against the tree data. Changing it is a one-line edit, a rebuild, and a new
`targetHeightM` in the manifest.

Note that on a 6.1 m slope the tallest tree is not the highest tree: `max_z` (16.86 m)
belongs to a 14.34 m plane standing uphill at u +68, not to the 15.00 m elm at u +33.5.
The validator checks both, separately.

## Trees

Species mix from HortScience, *Tree Assessment, South Park*, March 2012 / updated
November 2015 (31 London plane, 13 American elm, 3 silver dollar gum, 3 white alder,
1 Lombardy poplar, 1 olive, of 52 assessed), reconciled with the 2016–17 works: 30
removed, 24 mature 36″ box trees planted, "18 aged elm and sycamore" retained.

- The **20 OSM-mapped positions are the retained mature stand** and are exact. The three
  silver dollar gums are documented as being "on the north side of the park" and go to
  the three lowest-`v` mapped trees; the remaining 17 split 7 elm / 10 plane by a
  deterministic hash, in roughly the 13:31 ratio the survey found.
- The **14 derived trees are the one place this asset departs from measured data.** OSM
  maps 20 trees; the Chronicle records 18 retained plus 24 planted, and the aerial shows a
  near-continuous perimeter canopy that 20 crowns cannot produce. The rule is stated in
  `derive_infill()`: one tree at the midpoint of every gap longer than 14 m along each of
  the two long garden-bed edges (|v| > 6 m), inset to the mean |v| of the mapped edge
  trees. Nothing is placed in the middle of the park. Every derived position is tagged
  `"src": "derived"` in `data/park_uv.json`. They are all the low-broadleaf family — a
  2017 planting eight years on.
- Four silhouette families, differentiated by proportion and height rather than by leaf
  (style bible §12): elm (tall vase, 13.5–15.0 m), London plane (broad pollard,
  10.0–12.5 m), silver dollar gum (higher branch point, 12.0–14.0 m), low broadleaf
  (6.0–7.5 m). The trunk reaches **into** the crown in every family.

## Night state

The **path is the hero**: one continuous lit curve threading a dark canopy, which is what
the 2017 lighting scheme does on the ground. The four measured lamp heads are the only
supporting accents. Lawns, beds and crowns go dark. Every glow surface is a thin shell
proud of an opaque parent — the app renders `_Glow` at ~12% alpha by day, so a primary
surface authored as glow would vanish in daylight. The glow is one ribbon rather than one
shell per tablet: 72 shells cost 2,000 triangles and read at night as a dashed line.

## Semantic exaggerations, and why

1. **Tablets at 2.6 m** instead of the real ~1.2 m — the popsicle-stick rhythm is a
   recognition cue and 1.2 m pavers are ~2 px at the app's camera (style bible §9).
2. **The Shout's tubes at 0.45 m diameter** instead of ~0.15 m, so the circle reads at all.
3. **The kerb 0.70 m wide and 0.16 m proud** — the oval outline is recognition cue #1 and
   a scale-accurate kerb does not draw it from above.
4. **Crowns generously sized** so the perimeter reads as the continuous canopy the aerial
   shows, from only 34 trunks.
5. **Furniture ~15% oversized** for scale-cue legibility (§14).

## Corrections to the plan

- Plan 2.7 puts the play mound in `Toy_sand`. **Built in `Toy_mint`**: the OSM playground
  way is tagged `landcover=grass, surface=grass`, so it is the park's fifth lawn, and only
  the fall zone under the Shout is surfaced.
- Plan 2.7/2.8 gives the beds `Toy_teal` and the crowns `Toy_verdigris`. **Built with both
  in `Toy_verdigris`** — see REPORT for the two rejected alternatives.
- Plan 2.12 predicts `dims [117.11, 115.74, 15.0]`. **Built 122.46 × 121.05 × 15.00** —
  the plan's figure was the bare ring; the canopy overhangs the kerb, as real canopy does.
- Plan 2.14 asks for the Shout circle at 11.8 m and a crest of 4.34 m. Those are
  **centreline** figures; a bounding box measures the outer envelope, which is half the
  gauge plus one tube radius wider and one tube radius taller. The validator converts
  rather than loosening the tolerance.
- Plan 2.11 budgets ~12,000 triangles across a slightly different breakdown; the shipped
  split is in `REPORT.md`.
