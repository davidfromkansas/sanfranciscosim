# Powell Street cable car — reference dossier

Research for the SF-SIM miniature asset `cable-car-powell`. Compiled
12 August 2026 by independent verification of `docs/asset-plans/transit/cable-car.md`
§2, not by trusting it. Where this dossier and the plan disagree, the
disagreement is called out and the evidence given.

No copyrighted imagery is reproduced here. Photographs were examined on
Wikimedia Commons (CC-licensed, geolocated at the Powell–Mason turnaround at
Bay & Taylor) and are cited by file name.

## 2. Verified dimensions

| Item | Value | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **8.40 m** (27 ft 6 in) | Wikipedia, Cable Car Museum, MSR — all three |
| Width | **2.40 m** (8 ft) | all three |
| Height | **3.15–3.18 m** (10 ft 4 in – 10 ft 5 in) | Museum 10 ft 4 in, MSR 10 ft 5 in — the plan's "~3.1 m *inferred*" is confirmed and tightened |
| Weight | 7,000 kg (15,500 lb) | all three |
| Capacity | 60 total / 29 seated | all three |
| Fleet | 28 single-ended Powell cars | Wikipedia, SFMTA |
| Track gauge | **1,067 mm** (3 ft 6 in) | Wikipedia |

The model uses 8.40 × 2.40 × 3.18 m and a 1.067 m wheel gauge. The 10 ft 5 in
figure was taken over 10 ft 4 in because MSR measures a specific rebuilt car and
the cars genuinely differ — MSR notes each has "been rebuilt many times".

## 3. Observations from every side

### Front (the gripman's end)

A low **dash panel** roughly waist height, cream/white with a maroon border band
top and bottom, carrying the car number in gold and a yellow route board
("POWELL AND MARKET / BAY & TAYLOR / FISHERMANS WHARF"). A single round
**headlamp** fitting sits on the dash, offset from the board rather than
centred. Below the dash: a black fender and the black underframe. Above the
dash the car is **open** — you see straight through the grip section, past the
gripman, to the enclosed cabin behind.

### Sides

Three distinct zones, front to rear:

1. **Open grip section**, roughly 3 m. No side wall above the seat line. The
   roof is carried on four slim varnished-wood posts per side with **curved
   decorative brackets** at the post heads. Inside, back-to-back
   **outward-facing benches**: a longitudinal back down the centreline with a
   seat pan each side, so riders face out to the kerb.
2. **Enclosed cabin**, roughly 3.8 m, with **four arched-top windows** per side
   in varnished wood frames, a maroon panel below the sill carrying a gold
   pinstripe rectangle and the car number, and the sky-blue rocker band below
   that lettered SAN FRANCISCO MUNICIPAL RAILWAY.
3. **Rear platform**, roughly 1.2 m, open-sided, with a folding accordion gate
   at the cabin end and the conductor's position.

A **running board** runs the full length outboard of the body at roughly knee
height, with plates reading TWO STANDEES ONLY on its face. **Grab poles** rise
from the board to the roof edge at every post. In the photographs the poles are
**dark**, not brass — see §6.

### Rear

A full-width solid **dash** taller than the front one: cream field, maroon
border bands lettered MARKET STREET & above and FISHERMANS WHARF below, the
striped destination board in the centre, the car number in gold, and a
red-and-yellow DO NOT PASS CABLE CAR plate. Small red lamps at the roof corners
and at the underframe. Above the dash the rear platform is **open**, and the
straight-on rear photograph shows daylight through the whole car.

### Roof

A gently crowned main roof with a **maroon fascia band** at the eaves, carrying
a raised **monitor (clerestory) deck** that runs most of the length with arched
ends. The monitor's flanks carry the long **"POWELL & MASON Sts." letterboard**
over the open half and small clerestory lights over the enclosed half. A gong
is mounted on the main roof near the front.

**This settles the plan's open question** (`cable-car.md` §2.9, "Roof clerestory
unconfirmed"): the monitor deck is **present**, it is the roof's only real
feature, and it is what carries line identification.

### Night

Warm incandescent interior light visible *through* the car — through the open
sections directly and through the cabin's arched windows. The destination
boards are internally lit. A single forward headlamp.

## 4. The disagreement with the plan: "open sections at both ends"

`cable-car.md` §1 and the task prompt both say the Powell car has "open sections
at both ends". **It does not, in the same sense at both ends.** Wikipedia,
Market Street Railway's operations description and all four photographs agree:

- the **front** is a full open section with the gripman and outward-facing benches;
- the **rear** is a *small open platform* behind an enclosed cabin.

The model is built to the sources, not to the plan: a 3.07 m open grip section,
a 3.80 m enclosed cabin, a 1.17 m open rear platform. The plan's underlying
intent survives intact — **both ends are see-through** — and the correction
makes the vehicle more, not less, recognisable, because the long enclosed
cabin with its arched windows is half the Powell car's silhouette.

The plan's related claim that this "covers two of the three lines —
Powell–Mason and Powell–Hyde — and 28 of the system's 40 cars" is confirmed by
SFMTA and Wikipedia.

## 6. Where the model departs from the plan's §2.7 recipe, and why

| Plan says | Built as | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `Toy_brick` #c96f4a for the body | **`Toy_maroon` #7b2230** (off-palette WARN) | `Toy_brick` is a warm terracotta that reads as a brick building. The plan itself authorises taking the WARN: "the livery is a substantial part of this asset's identity and is worth the warning" |
| Grab poles `Toy_gold` | **`Toy_ink`** | The photographs show dark stanchions, not brass. It also helps: dark verticals against a bright background are what make the open sections read as open in the backlit test. Brass is kept where it is real — the roof moulding, the handrails, the lamp bezel, the gong, the pinstripes |
| Trucks: "two trucks, 4 wheels total" | one **rigid four-wheel truck**, 3.20 m wheelbase | The photographs show a single rigid frame, not two bogies. Gauge honoured at 1.067 m as the plan asks |
| Roof `Toy_roofd` (dark grey) | **`Toy_sand` cream over a `Toy_maroon` fascia** | Photo-verified. A dark roof would be wrong and would also waste the one surface the 42° camera looks straight at |
| Glow set of three surfaces | **four** — lit cabin panes added | Without them the enclosed half of the car goes black at night while the open half glows, which is the opposite of the plan's own stated goal for the night state |
| Varnished wood not in the palette | **`Toy_oak` #c08e50** (off-palette WARN) | The wooden read is half of why the vehicle looks 19th-century |

## 7. Axis convention, verified rather than assumed

`docs/asset-plans/transit/README.md` states the vehicle front as "−Z **in
Blender**", which cannot be right — Blender −Z exports to glTF −Y, straight
down. The authoritative statement is `vehicles_manifest.json`: *"meters, Y-up,
base sits on ground (minY=0), origin centered in X/Z footprint, FRONT = −Z"*,
i.e. the convention is stated in **glTF space**.

Verified empirically rather than reasoned: `commuter-bus.glb` was re-imported
into Blender and its windshield (`Toy_Glass`, high, full width) sits at
**Blender +Y = +5.22**, with min Z = 0.014. Blender's exporter maps
(x, y, z) → (x, z, −y), so Blender **+Y** is glTF **−Z**.

This asset is therefore authored with the gripman's end toward **Blender +Y**
and min Z = 0, and `validate_cable_car.py` reports every figure converted back
into glTF space so the numbers can be compared with the manifest directly.

## 8. The 3–5 strongest recognition cues, ranked

1. **You can see through it.** Voids at both ends, gaps between roof posts,
   no wall where every other vehicle in the city has one.
2. **Riders standing on the running boards**, outboard of the body, holding
   vertical poles — the shape extends past the vehicle.
3. A **tiny maroon-and-cream wooden box**, 8.4 m against a 12.2 m bus.
4. The **monitor roof** with its long pale letterboard.
5. The **gripman standing** at the levers in the open front section.

## 9. Preserve / simplify

**Preserve** — real 8.4 m length; the open grip section and rear platform; the
running boards at 0.54 m with figures on them; the monitor deck; the band
livery; the 1.067 m gauge.

**Simplify** — window arches become a chamfered top; the panel mouldings become
one gold pinstripe rectangle; the accordion gate becomes one dark slatted
panel; the grip becomes a lever, a housing and a shank reaching for a street
with no slot in it; lettering becomes dark bars on light boards at the size the
words would occupy; figures are chunky closed-box silhouettes with no faces and
no fingers. Grab poles are **thickened to 45 mm radius against a real ~20 mm**
so they survive at 120 m.

## 10. Uncertainties

- **Livery varies by car.** Cars 3, 13, 15 and 16 wear heritage schemes on
  purpose. The model uses the fleet standard, which the majority of the 28 cars
  carry.
- **Height 10 ft 4 in vs 10 ft 5 in.** Two reputable sources, 25 mm apart, on
  vehicles that are individually rebuilt. 3.18 m used.
- **Wheelbase not published.** 3.20 m is scaled from the photographs against
  the known 8.40 m length; treat as ±0.3 m.
- **The California car's livery is still unverified** — the deferred variant in
  `cable-car.md` §2.16 should not assume it matches the Powell scheme. SFMTA's
  page describes the California cars as "larger maroon vehicles", which is
  suggestive but not a specification.
